The problem

Citations get copied. Claims get stretched.

Students and researchers frequently cite papers they have not read in full. A figure gets rounded up, a crucial limitation disappears, or a superseded study remains in the bibliography. Checking every reference manually takes hours.

What the manuscript claims (illustrative) EXAGGERATED SCOPE
The treatment cut symptoms by 60% [12].
What the cited paper actually reports (illustrative) PRIMARY SOURCE [12]
Symptoms fell by 18% in the treated group, with wide variation across subgroups.

Features

Built for evidence, not guesses

Everything you need to audit a bibliography, with complete transparency into how every verdict was reached.

Claim extraction

Isolates statements that rely on external citations so you focus review time where it matters.

Source retrieval

Locates each cited paper and scans the relevant sections, tables, and conclusions.

Quoted evidence

Every verdict links directly to the verbatim passage and page metadata in the original publication, letting you verify context in seconds.

Honest uncertainty

Confidence scores accompany every result. When source access is paywalled or evidence is ambiguous, CiteVerify flags it explicitly instead of guessing.

Outdated source alerts

Flags references that have been retracted, superseded, or aged out for fast-moving fields.

Human review

Confirm or override any verdict and attach reviewer notes. Researchers remain in control.

Shareable reports

Export a structured PDF audit report containing verdicts, quoted passages, and open items.

Verdicts

Six outcomes, clearly explained

Each claim receives a standardized classification paired with a confidence level and written rationale.

SUPPORTED

The cited paper contains direct evidence that accurately backs the claim as written.

PARTIALLY SUPPORTED

The paper supports part of the claim, but overstates its magnitude, scope, or certainty.

UNSUPPORTED

No passage or dataset in the cited paper substantiates the stated claim.

CONTRADICTORY

The cited paper reports findings that directly conflict with the claim.

POTENTIALLY OUTDATED

The cited source may have been superseded by newer consensus or follow-up trials.

NEEDS REVIEW

Evidence is inconclusive or inaccessible. Human inspection is required.
